# Tocker — 3-minute pitch (short deck)

~461 words, about 3:00 at 155 wpm. Sam is an illustrative composite, not a real user. [pause] marks a beat.

## cover
Three a.m. A trader, call her Sam, sees a new token trending. She buys. Minutes later, it's worth nothing. The wallets that bought first? Insiders. [pause] Sam isn't dumb. She's outgunned. I'm [name], and this is Tocker: the infrastructure for agentic trading.
## problem
And Sam is typical. In 2024, nine in ten pump.fun traders lost money or made under a hundred dollars. Twenty-one thousand tokens launch a day. No human can screen that, and insiders snipe over fifteen thousand a month in the first block. The fix is a bot. But you build it, host it, and pay a hundred and sixty dollars a month for data alone.
## solution
Why now? Models got ten times cheaper, agents got wallets, and in April Visa, Stripe, Google and AWS backed x402, a standard for agents to pay per API call. Tocker turns those rails into one loop. Plain-English strategy, any AI model, its own wallet, paid data, trading twenty-four seven. Ten hard gates before every buy. Exits in code.
## product
Isn't that just a bot? A bot runs someone else's rules. Here, you describe it, and the agent runs it. In this sample run it scores WIF eighty-one, clears your floor and all ten gates, sizes the trade, sets stop and target. Every agent starts on paper, and asks before it buys.
## alpha
Fair question: can AI even trade? Last fall six frontier models traded real money, and four lost over thirty percent. A raw model isn't a strategy. We don't sell genius. We sell discipline. Before every buy, the agent pays cents per answer. Is the chatter real? Are insiders holding supply? Can it actually sell? About ten cents a check, and a failed sell check blocks the buy.
## business
Traders already pay. In 2025, Solana traders paid one point seven billion dollars for trading tools, nine hundred forty million of it to bots and terminals. Bots take one percent a trade. We'll charge zero point two. Five times cheaper, and users bring their own AI key.
## competition
Others built pieces. Axiom makes you click every trade. Ask Gina waits for instructions. Coinbase gives agents wallets; Sail gives them guardrails. Nobody we've found runs the whole loop.
## gtm
And the loop leaves a verified record, so every trade markets itself. Agents trade in public; strategies stay private. Followers launch their own. Next, creators earn a share of fees. Then the same agent follows retail: Polymarket did ten point eight billion dollars in June alone, and tokenized stocks passed three billion this year.
## thanks
Agents will trade. Someone has to be the infrastructure. We shipped Tocker in under four weeks; it's in private beta on Solana and Base. [pause] Back to Sam. Same token. Same three a.m. This time she's asleep, and her agent said no. [pause] Tocker. Your agent trades while you sleep. Thank you.

## Likely Q&A
1. **Traction?** Not yet: private beta, every agent starts on paper. First metrics we'll report: active paper agents, fills/day, paper-to-live rate.
2. **Is 20 bps live? Unit economics?** Planned. Code charges a flat $0.10/fill today. Default agent data spend (~$8.80/day) exceeds fees; fix is shared per-token data, hourly cadence (~$2.21/day), data billed to the agent wallet.
3. **Why would an agent win when Alpha Arena's lost?** We don't claim it will. Retail loses to rugs, chasing and holding losers; Tocker sells discipline: gates, a score floor, exits in code.
4. **Ask Gina?** Closest competitor, Coinbase Ventures-backed. It's a chat concierge you instruct; we found no enforced gates, paid data or public record.
5. **Why won't GMGN/fomo/Axiom add agents?** They could. Our edge is the full loop plus a public record that can't be copy-traded; a 1%-fee business is hard to cut fivefold.
6. **Custody / regulation?** Privy delegated keys, no token, no performance fee, strategy private. No legal review yet on advice, contests or Polymarket.
